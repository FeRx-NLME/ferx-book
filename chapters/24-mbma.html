Model-based meta-analysis

Where you are

A model-based meta-analysis (MBMA) fits a dose-response and time-course model to published summary data: one mean per study arm and visit, with its standard error and the number of patients behind it. There are no individual patients. Each row counts according to its precision, and arms differ from one another more than their sampling error alone explains.

This chapter fits the bundled mbma_placebo example. It covers the arm-level data shape and ferx_mbma_data(), inverse-variance weighting with weight = on the error model, between-arm variability as a weighted kappa, an unstructured placebo time course as a theta level block, and how to read, save and resample such a fit.

ImportantNeeds ferx-r newer than 0.4.0

This chapter needs the build the book is pinned to (Installing ferx). ferx-r 0.4.0 has no ferx_mbma_data() (nor print() for its result), no mbma_placebo example, no fit$theta_levels and no ferx_contrast_vpc() (nor print() or plot() for its result). Fitting a data.frame, the .fitrx round trip of a level block, the standard error and SIR interval of each level, ferx_sir() / ferx_covariance() following the fit’s own settings, a profiled level block (profile, fit$profiled_blocks, bounds on it and fit$profiled_levels_at_bound), nested variability levels (level, fit$kappa_levels, fit$ebe_kappas_by_level, the nested_levels example) and a within-arm residual correlation line are newer than 0.4.0 as well.

NoteMaturity

The weight = modifier, on the error model and on a kappa, is experimental in ferx-core (residual weighting, sample-size-weighted IOV). So is the residual correlation line. Theta level blocks are documented on the parameters page.

The columns follow one convention:

  • ID and STUDY are the study. A random effect on ID is therefore between-study variability.
  • OCC is the arm within the study, numbered from 1. A kappa, the random effect that is drawn once per occasion, is therefore between-arm variability.
  • DV is the reported arm mean, SE its standard error and NARM the number of patients in the arm. DOSE is the arm’s dose; placebo arms have 0.
  • There are no dose records. The model is compartment-free: DOSE is a covariate, not an amount.

The arguments:

  • data is the arm table. study, arm, time, mean and n name its columns.
  • Give exactly one of se (the standard error of the arm mean) or sd (the between-patient SD, converted to sd / sqrt(n), as the message above says).
  • scale is "continuous" (the default), "proportion" or "percent". The last two check the range of mean, and "percent" divides mean and se by 100.
  • covariates carries further columns through. Character and factor columns are coded as integers, with the codes in attr(d, "codes").
  • levels adds a LEVEL_IDX column that numbers the observed combinations of the named columns, for a counted level block theta NAME[N]. index takes your own numbering instead, for example to pool visits into windows. This chapter’s model uses the data-driven form, which needs neither.

Minimal runnable call

The model has four parts: a typical placebo level TVE0 with a between-study random effect ETA_E0, a placebo time course PLACEBO with one level per study and visit, an Emax dose-response that builds up over time, and a between-arm random effect KAPPA_ARM:

invisible(ferx_model_get_section(mbma$model, "parameters"))
#> # [parameters]
#>   theta TVE0(45.0, 0.0, 200.0)      # mean placebo level over a study's visits
#>   theta EMAX(8.0, 0.0, 100.0)       # maximum drug effect (score points)
#>   theta ED50(20.0, 0.1, 1000.0)     # dose at half-maximal effect (mg)
#>   theta ET50(2.0, 0.01, 100.0)      # time to half-maximal drug effect (weeks)
#>   theta PLACEBO[STUDY, TIME, contrast = sum_to_zero_within](0.0, -50.0, 50.0)
#> 
#>   omega ETA_E0 ~ 4.0 (sd)           # between-study variability on TVE0
#>   kappa KAPPA_ARM ~ 4.0 (sd) weight = NARM

Placebo and treated arms are fitted together, in one likelihood: all 22 arms of the 6 studies, 6 on placebo and 16 on an active dose. Every arm of a study shares that study’s placebo levels, and the Emax term, which is zero at DOSE = 0, adds the drug effect on top. The model therefore assumes that the placebo response is the same in a study’s placebo and treated arms and adds to the drug effect. Within a study, the difference between a treated arm and its placebo arm is what identifies EMAX, ED50 and ET50.

Reading the result

Residual weighting: weight = on the error model

The error model is DV ~ additive(ADD_ERR) weight = SE, with ADD_ERR fixed at a variance of 1. weight = W multiplies the additive residual SD by W, so each row’s residual SD is its own reported standard error, and the row counts 1 / SE^2 in the objective. This is inverse-variance weighting. The weight is a property of the data row, so it may use covariates, TIME and thetas, but not random effects or predictions.

DV, PRED, IPRED and simulations all stay on the natural scale, so nothing has to be transformed back before plotting. The individual weighted residual is the residual divided by that row’s standard error:

head(fit$sdtab[, c("ID", "TIME", "DV", "IPRED", "IWRES")], 4)
#>   ID TIME     DV    IPRED      IWRES
#> 1  1    0 49.630 49.35917  0.2544192
#> 2  1    2 46.720 47.10682 -0.3853785
#> 3  1    8 46.387 46.24111  0.1440162
#> 4  1   12 43.638 44.16774 -0.4537883
all.equal(fit$sdtab$IWRES, (arm_data$DV - fit$sdtab$IPRED) / arm_data$SE)
#> [1] TRUE

Keep sigma fixed at 1 when the weights are the reported standard errors of a continuous endpoint: the weight already carries the whole precision, and a free sigma would count the scale twice. The ferx-core error model page describes the other convention, a free sigma for a weighted logit of a responder proportion.

Between-arm variability: the weighted kappa

Two arms of the same study at the same dose would still not give the same mean: arms differ by more than their standard errors. That between-treatment-arm variability is smaller for a larger arm. The model declares it on the kappa:

kappa KAPPA_ARM ~ 4.0 (sd) weight = NARM

weight = NARM means the kappa of an arm with NARM patients is drawn from a normal distribution with variance γ² / NARM. ferx estimates γ², the variance for an arm of one patient, which is the quantity a published MBMA reports. Printed on its own, its SD looks large, so the fit prints the SD at the typical arm beside it, on the line that starts weight = NARM. The same numbers are in the fit:

So the convention for a report is: give the estimate as the unweighted γ² (156, standard error 64.2), and print beside it the effective between-arm SD at the median arm, 1.00 score points at NARM = 155. The generator used γ² = 100, inside the estimate’s 95% interval. With 22 arms the median falls between two arm sizes. R’s median() averages them (154.5), while ferx takes an observed arm size, so read the typical arm from fit$kappa_weight_typical rather than recomputing it.

KAPPA_ARM enters BASE additively, so fit$kappa_param_types is additive, and the print shows an SD rather than a CV%. The kappa’s shrinkage, fit$shrinkage_kappa, is a fraction (0.196), not a percentage.

TVE0 is each study’s mean placebo level

PLACEBO[STUDY, TIME] gives every study and visit its own placebo level, so no parametric placebo curve can bias the drug effect. Together with TVE0 and the study random effect ETA_E0, those levels have one degree of freedom too many per study: a study’s level could move up and its ETA_E0 down by the same amount. The block’s contrast = sum_to_zero_within removes it by making each study’s levels sum to zero.

That convention changes what TVE0 means. TVE0 + ETA_E0 is a study’s mean placebo level over its own visits, not its week-0 value, and the placebo levels are deviations from that mean. In this example the placebo score improves over time, so a study’s week-0 placebo level tends to lie above its mean:

The generator’s nominal week-0 level is 50, but that is not what TVE0 estimates. The truth to compare with is the realised mean placebo level over the six studies, 48.19, which the generator printed above. The estimate, 48.58 (standard error 0.95), is close to it. The drug parameters are estimated with the generator’s values inside their 95% intervals:

Correlated residuals within an arm

The model scores an arm’s visits as independent once its random effects are known: the kappa shifts every visit of an arm by the same amount, and what is left is independent. Arm means of one study arm over time can be correlated beyond that, for instance because the same patients are measured at every visit. A correlation line in [error_model] lets the residuals of the rows of one group correlate. Its parameter is an ordinary theta that only the line reads.

Compound symmetry, cs, correlates every two rows of a group equally, and on this model that is the same covariance as the arm kappa. ferx therefore refuses cs grouped by the arm while a kappa is drawn per arm:

Checking the treatment contrast

An MBMA is about the treatment contrast: the difference between an active arm and placebo within a study. An arm-level VPC (Simulation-based evaluation: VPC) mixes that with everything else the arms of a study share, above all the between-study spread. ferx_contrast_vpc() forms the contrast directly. Within each study (within) and visit (time, TIME by default), it subtracts the reference arm (by equal to ref) from each active arm, on the data and on every replicate of a simulation from the fit. The between-study ETA_E0 and the placebo levels are shared by a study’s arms and cancel, so what is left is the drug effect plus the between-arm and residual variability. arm names the column that tells a study’s arms apart (OCC here):

Nested levels: sponsor, study and arm

So far each study is a subject: ETA_E0 is between-study variability, and the kappa, drawn once per arm, is between-arm variability. A meta-analysis can have one more grouping, such as several studies run by one sponsor, whose studies resemble each other more than studies of different sponsors. ferx declares such groupings as nested levels below the subject. The subject (ID) is the coarsest group, and level NAME by COLUMN in PARENT declares each finer one. Each kappa says at which level it is drawn, so a kappa at STUDY is shared by the arms of a study and a kappa at ARM is drawn once per arm.

The bundled studies have no sponsor. To show the layout, pair them into three sponsors (studies 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6). The pairing is arbitrary: the generator draws every study independently. ID becomes the sponsor, and ARM copies OCC. Level labels are nested by key, so arm 1 of study 1 and arm 1 of study 2 are different arms. A subject’s rows must be adjacent: the data are sorted by ID, as they would have to be after any other pairing:

nested_data <- arm_data |>

  arrange(ID, STUDY)
nested_data |> distinct(ID, STUDY) |> count(ID, name = "studies")
#>   ID studies
#> 1  1       2
#> 2  2       2

The placebo block stays as it is. Each level of PLACEBO[STUDY, TIME] lies inside one study, so the block could absorb a study kappa. ferx checks this when it binds the levels, and under sum_to_zero_within the block’s levels sum to zero within each study, which leaves each study’s mean to the kappa, as it left it to ETA_E0 before. Two models follow: the bundled model written with levels (the study kappa K_ST in place of ETA_E0, no sponsor effect), and that model with a sponsor random effect ETA_SP:

With no sponsor effect, the levelled model is the bundled model under another layout: the same objective, a study kappa equal to ETA_E0, the same arm γ² and the same placebo levels. One level below the subject is what iov_column already declared.

Adding ETA_SP lowers the OFV by 6.2 for one more parameter, and the sponsor variance (4.9) takes all of the between-study variance. The study kappa collapses to 0.000006, with a shrinkage of 99.7%, on the optimizer’s lower guard, and ferx says so with a parameter_at_runaway_guard warning. That is not evidence for a sponsor effect. The generator has none, and three sponsors are too few to estimate a variance: the drop is what this particular pairing of six study effects happens to give. Pairing studies 1-4, 2-5 and 3-6 instead gives no drop at all (an OFV difference of 0.000), with a sponsor variance of 0.000006. With real sponsors, judge such a split on more groups than this.

Saving and reloading the fit

A .fitrx file keeps the level layout: which data columns define the block, its contrast and its levels. A reloaded fit can therefore be used for predictions, simulations, SIR and covariance without the original R session:

fitrx_path <- file.path(mbma_dir, "mbma_placebo.fitrx")
ferx_save_fit(fit, fitrx_path)
fit_loaded <- ferx_load_fit(fitrx_path)
identical(fit_loaded$theta_levels, fit$theta_levels)
#> [1] TRUE
all.equal(fit_loaded$theta, fit$theta)
#> [1] TRUE
cov_loaded <- ferx_covariance(fit_loaded)
all.equal(cov_loaded$se_theta, fit$se_theta, tolerance = 1e-4)
#> [1] TRUE

The covariance step on the reloaded fit gives the same standard errors as the original fit, to the tolerance shown. Reproducibility and sharing covers .fitrx files in general.

Pitfalls

  • Simulate and predict on the fitted cells only. The placebo levels exist for the study-by-visit cells in the fit data. A visit or a study the fit never saw has no level, and ferx refuses it rather than assume 0:

    new_visit <- arm_data[arm_data$STUDY == 1 & arm_data$OCC == 1, ][1, ]
new_visit$TIME <- 6
try(ferx_simulate(mbma$model, new_visit, n_sim = 1, fit = fit))
#> Error in ferx_rust_simulate_from_fit(model_path = normalizePath(model),  : 
#>   theta PLACEBO[STUDY, TIME]: the design has 1 level(s) the fit estimated no theta for: `STUDY=1,TIME=6`. A level's theta exists only for a combination the fit's data observed. `TIME` is a level column of this block, so the design can only be simulated at the fit's observation times; a denser or different time grid has no fitted theta. Either simulate only the fit's levels, or simulate the design without the fit's theta, from a theta vector for the design's own levels (the model's initial estimates, for example). [E_THETA_LEVEL_BINDING]

    To pool visits into windows, number the cells yourself with ferx_mbma_data(index = ...) or levels = and use the counted form theta PLACEBO[N].

  • A standard deviation is not a standard error. Pass a between-patient SD as sd = so ferx_mbma_data() divides it by sqrt(n). print() shows the SD the standard errors imply; it should look like a patient-level SD.

  • The OFV includes the weights. ferx scores on the natural scale, so the OFV differs from that of a model fitted to DV / SE by a term that depends only on the data. It cancels from every OFV difference between models on the same rows and weights. See the ferx-core error model page.

  • weight = needs an additive term. On a purely proportional error model the weight would cancel out, so ferx refuses it. A weighted kappa is likewise refused anywhere but [individual_parameters], where its scaling is applied:

    model_lines <- readLines(mbma$model)
proportional_lines <- sub("DV ~ additive(ADD_ERR) weight = SE", "DV ~ proportional(ADD_ERR) weight = SE",
                          model_lines, fixed = TRUE)
stopifnot(any(grepl("proportional(ADD_ERR) weight = SE", proportional_lines, fixed = TRUE)))
proportional_model <- file.path(mbma_dir, "mbma_proportional.ferx")
writeLines(proportional_lines, proportional_model)
try(ferx_fit(proportional_model, mbma$data, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error in ferx_rust_fit(model_path = normalizePath(model), data_path = normalizePath(data),  : 
#>   Error parsing model: [error_model] `weight = SE` has no effect on a purely proportional error model: weighting divides both DV and the prediction by the weight, and a common scale factor cancels out of a proportional (constant-CV) error. Use `additive(...)` or `combined(...)` — or drop the `weight =`. [E_PARSE]
kappa_lines <- sub("y    = BASE - DRUG", "y    = BASE - DRUG + KAPPA_ARM", model_lines, fixed = TRUE)
stopifnot(any(grepl("BASE - DRUG + KAPPA_ARM", kappa_lines, fixed = TRUE)))
kappa_model <- file.path(mbma_dir, "mbma_kappa_in_prediction.ferx")
writeLines(kappa_lines, kappa_model)
try(ferx_fit(kappa_model, mbma$data, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error in ferx_rust_fit(model_path = normalizePath(model), data_path = normalizePath(data),  : 
#>   Error parsing model: [structural_model] references `KAPPA_ARM`, a kappa declared with `weight = ...`. A weighted kappa is scaled where it is used, and only `[individual_parameters]` applies that scaling — a reference here would silently use the unweighted κ. Move the term into `[individual_parameters]` (assign it to an individual parameter and reference that name instead). [E_PARSE]

Warnings you may see here

No warning category has this chapter as its home. The fits above raise these, which other chapters explain:

  • inflated_rse (Diagnosing the model), because some placebo levels are small relative to their standard errors, which is expected for an unstructured placebo time course;
  • the threads note (Estimation methods and controlling the fit), on a machine with more cores than the subjects (six studies, three sponsors in the nested fits);
  • boundary_estimate (Diagnosing the model), on the profiled level held on its bound;
  • parameter_at_runaway_guard (Estimation methods and controlling the fit), on a variance that collapses to zero: the study kappa beside the sponsor effect, and the sponsor effect itself under the second pairing, which also raises eta_shrinkage (Diagnosing the model);
  • covariance_failed and covariance_step (Parameter uncertainty), from the "s" and "rsr" covariance steps.

The profiled fits can also carry a gradient_fallback note (Estimation methods and controlling the fit), W_PROFILE_FD_GRADIENT: a subject’s profiled levels got a finite-difference gradient instead of the exact one, which is correct but slower.

Summary

  • Arm-level data have one row per study, arm and visit; ferx_mbma_data() builds and checks that layout from a published table.
  • DV ~ additive(ADD_ERR) weight = SE with ADD_ERR fixed at 1 is inverse-variance weighting on the natural scale.
  • kappa ... weight = NARM is between-arm variability that shrinks with arm size. Report the unweighted γ² and the effective SD at the typical arm, sqrt(gamma2 / fit$kappa_weight_typical).
  • With contrast = sum_to_zero_within, TVE0 is each study’s mean placebo level over its visits, not its week-0 level.
  • A level block read linearly can be declared profile: the levels are solved at every step instead of searched, with the same objective, and estimates within a small fraction of their standard errors. Its bounds still apply, and a level held on one is listed in fit$profiled_levels_at_bound.
  • correlation car1(RHO) by OCC correlates an arm’s visits beside the arm kappa (cs there is refused). With few studies, calibrate its likelihood-ratio test by simulation.
  • fit$theta_levels holds every placebo level’s value and standard error, derived ones included, and its SIR interval after ferx_sir(sir_keep_samples = TRUE). Large blocks print as one THETA BLOCKS line.
  • level NAME by COLUMN in PARENT declares nested groupings below the subject, such as sponsor, study and arm, and kappa ... at LEVEL draws a kappa once per group of a level.
  • ferx_contrast_vpc() checks the model on the treatment contrast within a study, where the between-study variability cancels.
  • .fitrx files keep the level layout, and ferx_sir() and ferx_covariance() follow the fit’s settings.
