Experimental features

Where you are

Two model extensions in ferx are explicitly experimental: stochastic differential equations, which add random noise to the model states between observations, and neural networks for the covariate model, known as deep compartment models. Both fit from R with the bundled examples. This chapter runs them, compares them with their conventional counterparts, and shows what the fits report. It closes with parameter priors, which carry the same label without being a model extension.

WarningMaturity: experimental

ferx-core labels [diffusion] (SDE) and [covariate_nn] (neural networks) experimental: validated on a small set of examples, with syntax that may still change. Every fit that uses them carries an experimental warning. The feature maturity table’s third experimental entry is the Vern7 ODE stepper (Structural models: analytical and ODE). Parameter priors, at the end of this chapter, are labelled experimental on their own page but are absent from that table, and a priored fit raises no experimental warning.

Minimal runnable call: an SDE model

An SDE model is an ODE model with a [diffusion] block. Each line gives a state a diffusion variance, estimated as the parameter DIFF_<STATE>. ferx evaluates the likelihood with an extended Kalman filter, which adds the propagated state uncertainty to the residual variance at each observation:

invisible(ferx_model_get_section(sde$model, "odes"))
#> # [odes]
#>   # `central` is concentration (mg/L): the absorption term carries `/V` so the
#>   # ODE state matches the concentration-scale DV column. See the existing
#>   # `examples/bioavailability_ode.ferx` in ferx-core for the same convention.
#>   d/dt(depot)   = -KA * depot
#>   d/dt(central) =  KA * depot / V - (CL / V) * central
invisible(ferx_model_get_section(sde$model, "diffusion"))
#> # [diffusion]
#>   central ~ 0.01
fit_sde <- ferx_fit(sde$model, sde$data, verbose = FALSE)
fit_sde$uses_sde
#> [1] TRUE

Every relative standard error above runs to hundreds of percent or more (the smallest is 1,637%), for the structural parameters as much as for DIFF_CENTRAL. That is not what the data say about these parameters. The covariance step found a direction of negative curvature and floored it (covariance_regularized): the fit stopped at a saddle point, not at a minimum, and standard errors computed there mean nothing. [diffusion] needs finite-difference gradients, so the model file sets gradient_method = fd, and with it optimizer = auto resolves to bobyqa (Estimation methods and controlling the fit). The last sentence of the covariance_regularized message points at the ODE tolerances: the finite differences of the covariance step amplify the integration errors of the solver, and the message suggests tighter tolerances than the defaults:

regularized <- filter(ferx_get_warnings(fit_sde, as_df = TRUE), category == "covariance_regularized")
strwrap(sub(".*(Note that)", "\\1", regularized$message), 80)

#> [3] "1e-6 / ode_abstol = 1e-8 is on the measured accuracy plateau for this stencil"

With those tolerances the same model, optimizer and data reach an OFV 3.94 lower, and the covariance step needs no regularization. The standard errors are ordinary, except for the diffusion variance: at 2e-06 it cannot be told apart from zero. Where the default tolerances leave the fit depends on rounding in the solver, so the bundled fit’s OFV and estimates differ from one machine to the next; the refit is the one to read. Tighter default tolerances for finite-difference fits of ODE models are tracked in ferx-core #520.

Removing the [diffusion] block gives the ordinary ODE model. That model needs no finite differences: with gradient = "auto" ferx uses the analytic gradient. The table also fits it with the model file’s finite differences at the default tolerances:

The diffusion term does not improve on the ordinary ODE model. The SDE fit with the tighter tolerances has a diffusion variance of essentially zero and an OFV 0.12 above the ODE model’s analytic fit; at a zero diffusion variance the two models coincide, so even that SDE fit stopped slightly short. The ODE model’s own finite-difference fit also stops above its optimum, with a regularized covariance step: the trouble at the default tolerances comes from the finite differences, not from the diffusion term. These data show no system noise. A [diffusion] term is not a remedy for residuals that are correlated in time within a subject (Diagnosing the model). The filter propagates the state variance and adds it to the residual variance; the predicted state itself stays the ODE solution and is never pulled towards a subject’s observations, so the term cannot follow a drift. Here the Durbin-Watson statistic does not move: 2.61 with the block and 2.61 without. What [diffusion] does estimate is how much unexplained variance builds up between observations. The ferx-core page explains what the filter does not do.

Options that matter

Block or setting Meaning
[diffusion] STATE ~ variance Diffusion variance for an ODE state, estimated as DIFF_STATE (FIX to hold it)
[covariate_nn NAME] A neural network with inputs (covariates), outputs (parameter names), hidden layers, activation and output activation
NAME.OUTPUT in [individual_parameters] A network output, used like any other quantity

Constraints from the ferx-core diffusion page: [diffusion] needs an ODE model, cannot be combined with SAEM or [scaling] (Observation scaling with [scaling]), is estimated with FOCE or FOCEI, and uses finite-difference gradients.

Simulating from an SDE fit

Simulation from an SDE fit works like any other simulation (Simulating scenarios), here from the fit that reached the lowest OFV:

head(ferx_simulate(sde$model, sde$data, n_sim = 1, seed = 1, fit = fit_sde_tight), 3)
#> Warning: ferx_simulate produced 1 diagnostic:
#>   Stochastic differential equations ([diffusion] / Extended Kalman Filter) are an EXPERIMENTAL feature: validated only on a small set of toy examples, with estimator support limited to FOCE/FOCEI. Standard errors and convergence behaviour are not yet proven across diverse datasets — validate results carefully before relying on them. The filter is covariance-only: the state mean is propagated by the ODE and is never corrected by the observed data, so a [diffusion] fit scores the deterministic ODE prediction with an inflated observation variance rather than a filtered state. There is no filtered state trajectory, and sdtab IWRES from such a fit is scaled by the residual error alone, so it reads over-dispersed. See the Feature Maturity and SDE pages in the documentation.

How much the network departs from multipliers of 1 can be read from the individual parameters. For clearance, CL / (TVCL * exp(ETA_CL)) is the network’s multiplier for each subject:

subjects <- read.csv(dcm$data) |> distinct(ID, WT, CRCL)
# The network's clearance multiplier per subject: CL / (TVCL * exp(ETA_CL)).
clearance_multiplier <- function(f) {
  mutate(f$individual_estimates, ID = as.integer(ID)) |>
    select(ID, CL) |>
    left_join(mutate(f$ebe_etas, ID = as.integer(ID)) |> select(ID, ETA_CL), by = "ID") |>
    mutate(multiplier = CL / (f$theta[["TVCL"]] * exp(ETA_CL)))
}
multipliers <- left_join(clearance_multiplier(fit_dcm), subjects, by = "ID")
summary(multipliers$multiplier)

Regularizing the network

nn_l2 (weight decay) and nn_smooth (a penalty on curvature) keep a network from fitting spurious covariate effects. They are set in [fit_options] or with settings and apply to the FOCE-family methods and to VI; SAEM, importance sampling and Bayes do not apply them and say so in a warning. The penalty only shapes the search: the reported OFV stays the unpenalized one, so regularized and unregularized fits remain comparable (regularization). The same network with weight decay:

# suppress the R warning that reports overriding the model file's covariance setting
fit_dcm_l2 <- suppressWarnings(ferx_fit(dcm$model, dcm$data, covariance = FALSE,
                                        settings = list(nn_l2 = 0.01), verbose = FALSE))
data.frame(fit = c("network", "network, nn_l2 = 0.01", "two_cpt_oral_cov"),
           converged = c(fit_dcm$converged, fit_dcm_l2$converged, fit_cov$converged),
           parameters = c(fit_dcm$n_parameters, fit_dcm_l2$n_parameters, fit_cov$n_parameters),
           ofv = c(fit_dcm$ofv, fit_dcm_l2$ofv, fit_cov$ofv),
           aic = c(fit_dcm$aic, fit_dcm_l2$aic, fit_cov$aic))
#>                     fit converged parameters       ofv        aic
#> 1               network      TRUE        152 -1220.892  -916.8920
#> 2 network, nn_l2 = 0.01      TRUE        152 -1209.947  -905.9469
#> 3      two_cpt_oral_cov      TRUE         13 -1199.326 -1173.3264
multipliers_l2 <- clearance_multiplier(fit_dcm_l2)$multiplier
summary(multipliers_l2)
#>    Min. 1st Qu.  Median    Mean 3rd Qu.    Max. 
#>  0.9402  1.0234  1.0678  1.0534  1.0678  1.1271

With the penalty, the network’s clearance multipliers range from 0.94 to 1.13 across subjects, and the fit reports an unpenalized OFV of -1209.9, against -1220.9 without the penalty. A penalty cannot make the unpenalized objective better than its optimum, so whenever the penalized fit scores lower, the fit without it stopped at least that far above its optimum, although it reports convergence. Where an unpenalized network fit stops is fragile at this release: this one stops at a different point from one machine to the next, and ferx-core lists DCM FOCEI fits that report convergence far above a point of their own parameter space as a known issue (ferx-core #1561). Fit a network more than once, with and without a penalty, and compare the OFVs before reading anything into it.

Neither network fit beats the explicit covariate model on AIC (-916.9 without the penalty and -905.9 with it, against -1173.3): 152 parameters cost more than the objective gains.

Parameter priors

A prior pins a parameter near a value you already believe and lets the data move it only as far as the data can pay for – penalized maximum likelihood, the mechanism behind updating a published model on local data. It is written inline on the [parameters] row it belongs to, as a central value and a relative standard error, both on the scale the parameter was declared on:

warf <- ferx_example("warfarin")
priored <- file.path(experimental_dir, "warfarin_prior.ferx")
writeLines(sub("theta TVCL(0.134, 0.001, 10.0)",
               "theta TVCL(0.134, 0.001, 10.0) prior(0.25, rse = 10%)",
               readLines(warf$model), fixed = TRUE), priored)
fit_plain <- ferx_fit(warf$model, warf$data, verbose = FALSE)
fit_prior <- ferx_fit(priored, warf$data, verbose = FALSE)
c(no_prior = fit_plain$theta[["TVCL"]], with_prior = fit_prior$theta[["TVCL"]])
#>   no_prior with_prior 
#>  0.1329690  0.1627248

These data want a TVCL near 0.133; a prior centred at 0.25 with a 10% relative standard error pulls the estimate to 0.1627. The penalty is added to the objective, so fit$ofv is the penalized total and does not compare with the unpenalized fit. The fit reports the two halves separately: fit$ofv_data is the data’s share, fit$ofv_prior the penalty, and they sum to fit$ofv. The information criteria are built from the data half alone, so fit$aic is fit$ofv_data + 2 * fit$n_parameters and not fit$ofv + 2 * fit$n_parameters once a prior is in the model:

data.frame(fit = c("no prior", "prior on TVCL"),
           ofv = c(fit_plain$ofv, fit_prior$ofv),
           ofv_data = c(fit_plain$ofv_data, fit_prior$ofv_data),
           ofv_prior = c(fit_plain$ofv_prior, fit_prior$ofv_prior),
           aic = c(fit_plain$aic, fit_prior$aic),
           ofv_data_plus_2k = c(fit_plain$ofv_data + 2 * fit_plain$n_parameters,
                                fit_prior$ofv_data + 2 * fit_prior$n_parameters))
#>             fit       ofv  ofv_data ofv_prior       aic ofv_data_plus_2k
#> 1      no prior -280.3640 -280.3640    0.0000 -266.3640        -266.3640
#> 2 prior on TVCL -250.4404 -268.9709   18.5305 -254.9709        -254.9709

A fit without a prior has ofv_data equal to ofv and an ofv_prior of 0. ofv_data is the number to set beside the unpenalized fit: -268.97 against -280.36, so pulling TVCL towards 0.25 costs the data 11.39 points of objective, on top of a penalty of 18.53. print() and summary() state the split under the OFV line:

grep("OFV|prior", capture.output(print(fit_prior)), value = TRUE)
#> [1] " Model: warfarin_prior           Dataset: warfarin"                                             
#> [2] " OFV: -250.4404    AIC: -254.9709    BIC: -236.0676"                                            
#> [3] "  (prior on 1 parameter: data OFV -268.9709 + prior penalty 18.5305; AIC/BIC use the data half)"

fit$prior_summary has one row per priored parameter, and is NULL for a model without one:

fit_prior$prior_summary
#>   name prior_value  estimate shift_in_prior_sds penalty    family
#> 1 TVCL        0.25 0.1627248          -4.304706 18.5305 lognormal
#>   prior_lower_95 prior_upper_95
#> 1       0.205604      0.3039824
is.null(fit_plain$prior_summary)
#> [1] TRUE

shift_in_prior_sds is how far the estimate landed from the prior’s centre, with its sign, in prior standard deviations on the scale the prior is evaluated on. For a lognormal family that is the log scale, so it is not (estimate - 0.25) / 0.025. penalty is its square, and that parameter’s share of ofv_prior. prior_lower_95 and prior_upper_95 are the interval that rse = 10% implies for the family the engine chose (?ferx_fit describes every column). An estimate 4.3 prior standard deviations from the centre, outside that interval, says the prior and these data disagree. The standard errors of a priored fit are those of the penalized objective. What the prior cost in total is fit_prior$ofv - fit_plain$ofv, 29.92 points: the data’s share plus the penalty.

A [priors] block takes the priors from a previous fit instead of writing them out by hand. It has exactly one key, from_fit, naming a saved fit; from R that is the .fitrx bundle ferx_save_fit() writes. Every theta, omega diagonal and sigma of the saved fit that has a standard error, and whose name matches a parameter of the new model, gets prior(estimate, rse = SE / estimate): exactly the prior you would have typed off the earlier fit’s output. As an example, treat warfarin subjects 1 to 5 as an earlier study and subjects 6 to 10 as new data:

warf_data <- read.csv(warf$data, na.strings = ".")
earlier_data <- file.path(experimental_dir, "warfarin_earlier.csv")
later_data <- file.path(experimental_dir, "warfarin_later.csv")
write.csv(warf_data[warf_data$ID <= 5, ], earlier_data, row.names = FALSE, na = ".", quote = FALSE)
write.csv(warf_data[warf_data$ID > 5, ], later_data, row.names = FALSE, na = ".", quote = FALSE)

fit_earlier <- ferx_fit(warf$model, earlier_data, verbose = FALSE)
saved <- file.path(experimental_dir, "warfarin_earlier.fitrx")
ferx_save_fit(fit_earlier, saved)

# The model goes next to the saved fit, so from_fit can name it by a relative
# path, which is resolved against the model file's directory.
updating_model <- file.path(experimental_dir, "warfarin_from_fit.ferx")
writeLines(c(readLines(warf$model), "", "[priors]",
             paste0("  from_fit = ", basename(saved))),
           updating_model)
fit_later <- ferx_fit(warf$model, later_data, verbose = FALSE)
fit_updated <- ferx_fit(updating_model, later_data, verbose = FALSE)
fit_updated$prior_summary[, c("name", "prior_value", "estimate", "shift_in_prior_sds")]

Pitfalls

  • Experimental means validate yourself. Compare an SDE or network model with its conventional counterpart, as above, before relying on it.
  • Absolute from_fit paths. // starts a comment in a .ferx file, and R’s tempdir() contains one on macOS, so an absolute path built from it is cut short before the engine sees it. Keep the saved fit next to the model and give a relative path, as above; if you need an absolute one, write it with normalizePath(path, winslash = "/").
  • Extra flexibility is not extra information. A diffusion variance or a network with many weights needs data that support it; check standard errors, the OFV change and information criteria.
  • A converged network fit may be far from its optimum at this build. DCM fits can report convergence far above a point of their own parameter space (ferx-core #1561), and where warfarin_dcm stops without a penalty differs from one machine to the next. Compare fits with and without nn_l2.
  • Standard errors of network weights are not reliable, and the covariance step with many weights is slow. Skip it with covariance = FALSE while exploring.
  • SDE fits are slow. The extended Kalman filter uses finite differences; the warfarin SDE fit above took 6 times as long as the same model without [diffusion], both with finite differences.
  • Check that an SDE fit reached a minimum. With finite differences the default optimizer can stop at a saddle point, as the bundled fit does at this build. A covariance_regularized warning that reports negative curvature is the sign; refit with the ODE tolerances its message names, and compare OFVs.

Warnings you may see here

  • experimental (warning): the model uses an experimental component ([diffusion] or [covariate_nn]). The message states what has and has not been validated. It appears on every such fit.

Summary

  • [diffusion] adds system noise to ODE states, estimated with an extended Kalman filter. Check the covariance step, try another optimizer, and compare with the model without it.
  • [covariate_nn] lets a neural network produce covariate multipliers; compare its OFV and AIC with an explicit covariate model.
  • nn_l2 and nn_smooth regularize a network.
  • An inline prior(value, rse = …) regularizes a parameter towards a value you already believe, and [priors] from_fit builds such priors from a fit saved with ferx_save_fit(); the reported OFV is then penalized.
  • [diffusion] and [covariate_nn] fits carry an experimental warning.
